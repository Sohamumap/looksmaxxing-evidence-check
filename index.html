One claim, followed all the way

Do whitening strips work?

Mixed evidenceLow risk

  1. The promise

    Strips and trays that make your teeth whiter at home.

  2. What researchers tested

    A 2018 Cochrane review pooled 71 trials. In 26 of them, 1,398 adults got either a bleaching product or a placebo.

  3. What they found

    The bleaching products whitened teeth more than placebo over 2 weeks to 6 months. Side effects were mostly sensitivity and sore gums, mild and passing.

  4. What stays uncertain

    Anything past six months, and which product or strength is best. The reviewers rated the certainty low to very low: 67 of the 71 trials had an unclear risk of bias.

This page first said “strong”. That was wrong.

The first draft graded whitening “strong evidence” and filed it under Worth doing, because trial after trial shows whiter teeth. A reviewer pointed to the Cochrane review. Its authors read the same trials and rated the certainty low to very low.

The lesson: many trials agreeing is not strong evidence if the trials themselves are weak. The grade is now “mixed”.

EvidenceHow good the research is. Strong means repeated controlled trials in people.

RiskHow badly it can go wrong, and whether the damage can be undone.

Why two grades instead of one score

Because they come apart. Steroids build muscle and can wreck your heart. Sunscreen is about as safe as anything here and has a long trial behind it. One score would blur the two questions together.

How the grades were decided

"Strong" means repeated controlled trials in people. "Mixed" means a few small or inconsistent trials. "Weak" and "none" mean the claim rests on theory, anecdotes or before-and-after photos. Each practice has its own page with a named source, that source's limit, and what new evidence would change the grade.

Refreshed is not the same as reviewed

A script re-runs each PubMed search every week and updates the paper count on each page. That number shows how much research exists, not how good it is. If a search fails, the page keeps the last good number and says so. The grades were written once, on 11 October 2026, and are not re-checked automatically. No doctor has reviewed them.

What changed after the test, and what was wrong

In the 45-minute window: the 12 entries and grades, the two-grade idea, paper counts from PubMed, and eight named studies confirmed to exist and match their titles. They were not all read in full.

Later the same day: the layout was simplified because the first version was cluttered, and then redrawn as an illustrated field guide with one featured investigation.

After an outside review, three AI-drafted claims were checked against their sources and corrected:

Teeth whitening was graded "strong evidence". The 2018 Cochrane review rates the certainty low to very low, so it is now "mixed" and has moved out of "Worth doing".

Mewing was called "harmless". The American Association of Orthodontists names possible harms, so that word is gone.

Hard chewing was said to thicken the chewing muscles. The one randomised trial found no change in muscle thickness or jaw shape, so the entry now says that.

The other nine entries have not had the same check yet.
