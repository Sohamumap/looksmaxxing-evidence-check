Does it work? And what could it cost you?

Twelve things men try in order to look better, each given two separate grades: how good the evidence is, and how risky it is. They are separate on purpose. Steroids work and are dangerous. Mewing is safe and does very little.

Showing all 12.

Daily sunscreen (SPF 30+)

Strong evidenceLow risk

Do it. The cheapest thing on this list that is proven to slow skin ageing.

A randomised trial in Australian adults found people told to use sunscreen daily showed less skin ageing after 4.5 years than people who used it when they felt like it.

Watch out: Use enough of it and reapply outdoors. That is where most people fail.

What would change this grade: A long trial finding no difference in skin ageing between daily and occasional users.

Start with this study: Hughes et al., Annals of Internal Medicine, 2013. Sunscreen and prevention of skin aging: a randomized trial. Its limit: Adults under 55 in sunny Queensland, Australia. Results may differ for other skin types and climates.

Topical retinoids (tretinoin, adapalene)

Strong evidenceMedium risk

Works for acne and sun-aged skin. Start slowly.

Decades of controlled trials support retinoids for acne and for fine lines and uneven tone caused by sun damage.

Watch out: Peeling and redness in the first weeks are common. It makes skin more sun-sensitive. Not for use in pregnancy. Tretinoin needs a prescription in most countries.

What would change this grade: The benefit is settled. The risk grade would drop if a gentler form matched tretinoin in head-to-head trials.

Start with this study: Mukherjee et al., Clinical Interventions in Aging, 2006. Retinoids in the treatment of skin aging: an overview of clinical efficacy and safety. Its limit: A review article from 2006, not a new trial.

Topical minoxidil

Strong evidenceMedium risk

Works for male pattern hair loss, but only for as long as you keep using it.

Controlled trials show 5% minoxidil regrows or keeps more hair than placebo in male pattern hair loss.

Watch out: Early shedding is common. Scalp irritation happens. Stop and you lose the gains within months. Keep it away from cats, for whom it is poisonous.

What would change this grade: Long trials showing the gains last after you stop. Today they do not.

Start with this study: Olsen et al., Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, 2002. A randomized clinical trial of 5% topical minoxidil versus 2% topical minoxidil and placebo. Its limit: It ran for 48 weeks, so it does not show what happens over years.

Finasteride

Strong evidenceMedium risk

The best-studied pill for male pattern hair loss. A decision to make with a doctor.

Large long-running trials show it slows hair loss and regrows hair in most men who take it.

Watch out: Prescription only. A minority of men report sexual side effects, and some report low mood. Talk it through with a doctor rather than buying it from a grey-market seller.

What would change this grade: Better long-term data on how often side effects continue after stopping. That is the real open question.

Start with this study: Kaufman et al., Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, 1998. Finasteride in the treatment of men with androgenetic alopecia. Its limit: Men aged 18 to 41 with mild to moderate hair loss, in a study funded by the drug's maker.

Lifting weights and eating enough protein

Strong evidenceLow risk

Do it. The biggest visible change most men can make.

Resistance training reliably builds muscle and changes body shape, with strong evidence across ages and starting points.

Watch out: Progress is measured in months, not weeks. Learn the movements before adding weight.

What would change this grade: Nothing realistic. This is as settled as it gets.

Start with this study: Morton et al., British Journal of Sports Medicine, 2018. A systematic review and meta-analysis of protein supplementation and resistance training gains. Its limit: It pools many trials of different quality, and it is about protein supplements on top of training, not training alone.

Creatine monohydrate

Strong evidenceLow risk

One of the few supplements that does what it says.

Among the most studied supplements. It modestly improves strength and muscle gain when combined with training.

Watch out: Some early weight gain is water. Ask a doctor first if you have kidney disease.

What would change this grade: Long-term safety data in teenagers, who are under-studied.

Start with this study: Kreider et al., Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition, 2017. Position stand: safety and efficacy of creatine supplementation. Its limit: A position statement by a sports nutrition society, not a single trial.

Peroxide teeth whitening

Strong evidenceLow risk

Works on natural teeth. Follow the instructions.

Peroxide-based strips and trays whiten teeth in controlled trials.

Watch out: Tooth sensitivity and sore gums are common and usually pass. It does not change the colour of crowns or fillings.

What would change this grade: Evidence of lasting enamel damage at normal doses would raise the risk grade.

No single good study to point to. That absence is part of the grade.

Home microneedling for hair loss

Mixed evidenceMedium risk

Promising, not settled. Clinic-grade results do not transfer automatically to a roller at home.

A few small trials found microneedling plus minoxidil beat minoxidil alone. The studies are small and the methods vary.

Watch out: Infection and scarring if the tool is not sterile or you go too deep or too often.

What would change this grade: Larger trials using home rollers, not clinic devices, that also report infection rates.

Start with this study: Dhurat et al., International Journal of Trichology, 2013. A randomized evaluator blinded study of effect of microneedling in androgenetic alopecia: a pilot study. Its limit: A pilot study at one clinic, done by clinicians rather than at home.

Mewing (tongue posture)

Weak evidenceLow risk

Harmless, but no good evidence it reshapes an adult face.

There are no controlled trials showing that holding your tongue on the roof of your mouth changes adult jaw or cheekbone structure.

Watch out: The main cost is time and false hope. Before-and-after photos online mostly show weight loss, age, lighting and camera angle.

What would change this grade: One controlled trial in adults with before-and-after scans. None exists yet.

No single good study to point to. That absence is part of the grade.

Hard chewing and jaw trainers

Weak evidenceMedium risk

Little to gain, and a real chance of jaw pain.

Heavy chewing may slightly thicken the chewing muscles. There is no good evidence it changes jawbone shape.

Watch out: Jaw joint pain, clicking, headaches and tooth wear. Stop if your jaw hurts.

What would change this grade: A trial that measures jawbone, not just muscle, and tracks jaw joint pain.

No single good study to point to. That absence is part of the grade.

Steroids and SARMs for looks

Strong evidenceHigh risk

They build muscle. The price can be your heart, hormones and fertility.

The muscle-building effect is real. So is the damage, which is why this is graded on risk, not on whether it works.

Watch out: Heart disease, shutdown of your own testosterone, infertility, mood changes. SARMs are unapproved drugs, and products sold online often contain something other than what is on the label.

What would change this grade: Nothing on the harm. The open question is only how often it happens, and at what dose.

Start with this study: Baggish et al., Circulation, 2017. Cardiovascular toxicity of illicit anabolic-androgenic steroid use. Its limit: An observational study of long-term users, so it shows a link, not proof of cause.

Bonesmashing

No evidenceHigh risk

Do not do this.

There is no evidence that hitting your face makes bone grow in a better shape. Bone does not remodel that way.

Watch out: Fractures, nerve damage, lasting swelling and a lopsided face. If you have already done it and have numbness or pain, see a doctor.

What would change this grade: Nothing plausible. The claimed mechanism does not match how bone heals.

No single good study to point to. That absence is part of the grade.

How the grades work. "Strong" means repeated controlled trials in people. "Mixed" means a few small or inconsistent trials. "Weak" and "none" mean the claim rests on theory, anecdotes or before-and-after photos. Risk is graded on how badly it can go wrong and whether the damage can be undone.

Refreshed is not the same as reviewed. A script re-runs each PubMed search every week and updates the paper count. That number shows how much research exists, not how good it is.
