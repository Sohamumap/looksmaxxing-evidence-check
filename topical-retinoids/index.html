Does topical retinoids work, and is it safe?

Strong evidenceMedium risk

Short answer

Works for acne and sun-aged skin. Start slowly.

What the evidence says

Decades of controlled trials support retinoids for acne and for fine lines and uneven tone caused by sun damage.

What can go wrong

Peeling and redness in the first weeks are common. It makes skin more sun-sensitive. Not for use in pregnancy. Tretinoin needs a prescription in most countries.

What would change this grade

The benefit is settled. The risk grade would drop if a gentler form matched tretinoin in head-to-head trials.

Sources

Start with this study: Mukherjee et al., Clinical Interventions in Aging, 2006. Retinoids in the treatment of skin aging: an overview of clinical efficacy and safety. Its limit: A review article from 2006, not a new trial.
